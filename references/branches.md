# Branch contracts

Use only the section for the branch being generated. Do not import its material language into another branch.

## 1. `abstraction`

### Objective

Translate the photograph's dominant perceptual event into a complete abstract field. Preserve force, interval, rhythm, direction, expression, or structural tension rather than photographic appearance.

### Required decisions

- Choose one primary perceptual event.
- Preserve two to four relational anchors.
- Choose one dominant mark family and at most one supporting family.
- Retain the source color skeleton, then decide whether one minor source color deserves emphasis.
- Reserve enough quiet space for the dominant event to remain legible.

### Detail control

- Do not subdivide a small head, face, hand, or other small anchor into multiple planes. Use one stable mass plus at most one identifying interval.
- If expression is important, preserve its direction or asymmetry rather than reconstructing facial detail.
- If the source is non-figurative, substitute the equivalent structural anchor: horizon, opening, convergence, repetition, light gap, or mass relationship.
- Prefer a few large related forms over many small explanatory pieces.

### Title policy

An internal working title may summarize the current image and test semantic coherence. It must be newly derived, optional, invisible by default, and unable to override the observed source relationships.

### Avoid

Photographic pixels, literal tracing, complete silhouettes, tiny facial geometry, decorative symbols, excessive fragmentation, fixed palettes, fixed titles, and photo-plus-panel layouts.

## 2. `extend`

### Objective

Keep a truthful photographic field large enough to explain the scene, then transform removed or compressed regions into an original material extension.

### Photo allocation

- Preserve the core subject plus enough surrounding evidence to explain action, place, scale, or atmosphere.
- Choose among broad field, selective omission, directional fragment, or open aperture according to the source.
- Never default to a tight fitted cutout around the subject.

### Independent material language

- Select a transition from the source: pressure diffusion, tonal erosion, translucent overlap, cut aperture, field continuation, registration shift, or another source-derived handoff.
- A torn edge is optional, never mandatory.
- Extend one source axis, path, shadow, rhythm, atmospheric field, or material behavior. Do not reproduce a reference poster's signature arrangement.
- Use accent color only when it crosses, connects, replaces, redirects, or counterbalances a real source structure.
- Typography is optional and absent by default.

### Avoid

Reference-like vertical templates, obligatory cream paper, obligatory torn contours, generic orange trails, lower-right microtext by habit, tight masking, scrapbook decoration, and extensions that merely imitate the photo.

## 3. `reduction`

### Objective

Use the photograph as semantic evidence only and redraw a complete original paper artwork. Reduce photographic description while preserving the expressive mechanics, articulation, and material response that make the scene specific.

### Reduction floor and ceiling

- Preserve the semantic nucleus, dominant gesture, necessary subject articulation, one spatial cue, and one environment-response system.
- Remove logos, surface description, redundant background, realistic lighting, and photographic texture.
- Use one primary illustration grammar and at most one support grammar.
- Keep one environment-response system: snow pressure, water wake, wind field, shadow interval, architectural rhythm, or another source-derived reaction. One system may appear at several connected scales, such as a quiet ambient field, a directional path, and a localized contact response.
- Do not reduce the image to a generic silhouette plus a single line.
- Do not rebuild the entire scene or add several competing response systems.

### Tonal control

- Split a large dark subject with two or three adjacent neutral values, paper gaps, dry-ink erosion, or broken edges.
- Keep small anchors stable; do not facet a small head or face.
- Concentrate the darkest value at structural load points rather than filling the whole subject uniformly.
- Attach any high-chroma accent to a semantic pressure point, focal relationship, or directional handoff.

### Avoid

Photo pixels, tracing, sealed black slabs, excessive anatomy, over-segmentation, empty iconography, literal full-scene illustration, decorative metaphors, and typography added by default.
